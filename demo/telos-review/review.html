Key Result
KR 1: Handle time (FCR held)
Cut average handle time on dispute, fee and card contacts by at least 1 minute per contact, without lowering first-contact resolution.
Model: an AI copilot inside Nova Bank's desktop agent console (Egypt), laid out in three columns: queue, conversation with the copilot's draft, and the verified customer's records. It drafts a formal-Arabic reply with a citation on every claim, and only suggests actions that a human must click. Flow: a Franco-Arabic chat asking when a disputed EGP 1,200 comes back. Floor: EricaAssist's roughly 1 minute saved.

* Suggestions tied to the KRs

9
Start the draft when the case opens, stream the reply first, and keep Send locked until the checks pass. Run the copilot when the case is assigned, mark "Draft ready" on the queue card, stream the Arabic reply first, and turn on prompt caching ("cached 0" today). "Ask copilot" becomes regenerate. Nothing is sent until the agent clicks.
Handle time
How did I get this?
This answers rec 1 directly. Pre-generating moves the 15.75s off the agent's clock and into queue time. Streaming means even a draft that wasn't pre-generated is readable within a few seconds, and caching cuts processing time on every request. The agent stays in control because Send stays manual and locked until the checks pass. For calls, pair this with a short capture field or a live transcript so the draft doesn't wait for the agent to finish typing.
10
Lead with the answer, explain "provisional", and say what happens next. The reply pattern becomes: (1) "The EGP 1,200 has been in your account since 2 October as a provisional credit"; (2) one line from POL-04 §5 on what makes it permanent or reverses it; (3) the next step and when she'll hear back. Add a heads-up line to "For you" when the customer's words contradict the record, and add this case to the golden eval set.
FCR Handle time
How did I get this?
This answers rec 2 directly. Her literal question is "when?", and the true answer is "already, provisionally". Putting that first answers her in one turn, and explaining the reversal rule up front avoids a surprise later. The heads-up line gives the agent the one sentence to say on a call. Every fact comes from sources the copilot already cites (T00018 and POL-04 §5), so citation accuracy is unaffected.
11
Put the suggested action next to Send, and make the reply match what the agent actually did. When the agent clicks "Send for approval", change the closing line from an offer to a statement ("I have asked a supervisor to review your dispute; you will hear by <date>"). If the agent sends without requesting it, keep an "Escalation offered, not requested" marker. These stay two human clicks, and nothing runs automatically.
Handle time FCR
How did I get this?
This answers rec 3 directly. When the reply reports an action already requested, the "yes please" round-trip disappears. Keeping two clicks preserves the suggestions-only rule and the Team Leader approval, and the marker covers the forgotten offer seen on screen 06. The date in the statement must come from the policy SLA, not from the model.
12
Replace the toast with a pre-filled wrap-up card that closes the case in one click. After Send, add the sent message to the conversation, lock Send, and pre-fill the intent, D-1001, actions requested, the complaint flag with a CBE reference, and a two-line note. "Close and next" opens the next case with its draft waiting. After-call work turns from typing into checking.
After-call work Handle time FCR
How did I get this?
This answers rec 4 directly. Every wrap-up field already exists in the draft response: the intent tag, claims, actions and flags. Pre-filling them adds no new model work and removes typing from after-call work, the part of handle time this flow currently ignores. Registering the complaint in the same step meets the CBE 2-working-day reference rule and stops it coming back as a new contact. "Close and next", combined with pre-generated drafts, carries the time saved from one contact to the next.
